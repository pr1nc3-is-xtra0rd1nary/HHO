What is sunlight-driven photocatalytic water splitting?

This is an approach where light (usually sunlight) excites a photocatalyst material so it can drive chemical reactions that split water (H2O) into hydrogen (H2) and oxygen (O2) without electrical electrolysis. Photocatalysts absorb photons and create electron–hole pairs that participate in redox reactions at the catalyst surface. This is a research area in solar fuels and materials science.

Balanced chemical equation (molecular):

2 H2O → 2 H2 + O2

Why 2:1 hydrogen-to-oxygen? Each water molecule has two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. When two water molecules split, the hydrogen atoms pair up to make two H2 molecules (four H atoms total arranged into two H2 molecules) while the two oxygen atoms combine to make one O2 molecule — hence the 2:1 ratio by molecule count (H2 : O2).

    Safety guidance

    • Always work under qualified supervision when handling chemicals, gases, or reactions.
    • Hydrogen is flammable and forms explosive mixtures with air — never carry out gas collection without proper ventilation, gas-handling equipment, and trained staff.
    • Use appropriate PPE: safety glasses, gloves, lab coat. Know emergency procedures (fire extinguisher, gas shutoff, eyewash).
    • Do not attempt open-flame ignition or unauthorized testing. Contact your teacher and laboratory staff.

    This page is educational. It does not replace official laboratory safety training.
